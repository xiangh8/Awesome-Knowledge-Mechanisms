Knowledge Mechanisms Across the Lifecycle of Large Language Models

Following knowledge as a single object under continuous change — acquired, represented, utilized, revised, transferred, and evaluated.

Abstract

Knowledge is fundamental to the capabilities of large language models. Understanding how these models acquire, store, use, and revise knowledge has therefore become a central concern for interpretability, reliability, and safe deployment. Despite a rapidly growing of research, related studies remain scattered across communities with their own concepts, methods, and standards. There still lacks a unified view of how the mechanisms uncovered at different stages relate to one another.

In this survey, we systematically review knowledge mechanisms in LLMs by following knowledge through a lifecycle. Specifically, we organize existing studies into six interconnected stages, tracing how knowledge is acquired, how it is represented, how it is utilized and augmented, how it evolves through editing and unlearning, how it transfers across models, modalities, and languages, and how it is evaluated. Treating these stages as successive states of one underlying object brings their dependencies into view. Finally, we distill the main findings and open problems of each stage.

The lifecycle

Knowledge as one object, traced stage by stage

What a model acquires constrains what it can represent; how knowledge is represented governs how it is utilized; evolution revises what is already inside; transfer moves it beyond the model. Evaluation runs alongside them all.

Six stages

Each stage is a transformation of knowledge

Every stage studies a distinct transformation while inheriting constraints from the stages before it and reshaping the stages that follow.

01

Knowledge Acquisition 知识获取

Training methods, corpus composition, and architecture together decide what a model can ever know. They are interdependent, so they should be treated as one system rather than as separate knobs.

  • Training Methods
  • Training Data
  • Model Architecture
02

Knowledge Representation 知识表征

Where a fact resides in the parameters, and how it is instantiated as features during inference. The parametric and activation-space views are complementary rather than competing.

  • Parametric Storage
  • Knowledge Localization
  • Activation Space
03

Knowledge Utilization 知识利用

Models often hold factual signals that ordinary generation fails to surface — the encode–express gap. Many factual errors are access failures rather than gaps in what the model knows.

  • Knowledge Recall
  • Knowledge Augmentation
  • Knowledge Conflict
04

Knowledge Evolution 知识演化

Editing overwrites outdated facts and unlearning removes undesirable content. Because knowledge is distributed rather than stored as separate records, a local intervention can spread beyond its target.

  • Knowledge Editing
  • Knowledge Unlearning
  • Side Effects
05

Knowledge Transfer 知识迁移

Independently trained systems converge toward shared representational structure, and that convergence is what makes transfer possible. The barriers, by contrast, are specific to each boundary.

  • Cross-Model
  • Cross-Modal
  • Cross-Lingual
06

Knowledge Evaluation 知识评测

Parametric knowledge is reachable only through behavior, while contextual knowledge is directly checkable. Crossing the two sources with four properties yields a matrix in which the same property looks different on each side.

  • Factuality
  • Compositionality
  • Currency
  • Calibration

Takeaway

A model’s behavior is bounded not only by what it has learned, but by whether that knowledge can be reached and expressed. Failures that look like missing knowledge are often failures of access, arbitration, or propagation instead — and no single stage reveals the full extent of this gap on its own.
